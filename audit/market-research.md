# Market Research: Merchant-Risk and Transaction-Laundering Detection (incl. India)

Research date: 2026-11-19. Facts only, each with a source URL. Where a fact comes from a vendor's own marketing, it is labelled as a vendor claim. "Not found" means the search tooling did not surface a source for that point.

## Q1. Transaction-laundering detection vendors

- **LegitScript** uses "AI-enhanced" scanning of merchant websites for transaction-laundering indicators, plus human investigators who research beyond the website; it claims "nearly two decades" of merchant data (vendor claim). Source: https://www.legitscript.com/solutions/merchant-risk-solutions/transaction-laundering-detection/
- **LegitScript** says it looks beyond the merchant site through open-source research, and that transaction-laundering detection is included in its Merchant Monitoring product at no add-on cost (vendor claim). Source: https://www.legitscript.com/solutions/merchant-risk-solutions/merchant-monitoring/
- **LegitScript** states that acquirers using a merchant-monitoring partner can qualify for fine mitigation of 75% to 100% for BRAM or VIRP violations (vendor claim). Source: https://www.legitscript.com/bram-virp/
- **G2 Risk Solutions** Transaction Laundering Detection uses continuous portfolio analysis, network analysis, and test transactions to find payment paths between violating sites and merchants, with automated detection plus analyst validation (vendor claim). Source: https://g2risksolutions.com/merchant-risk/product/transaction-laundering-detection
- **G2 Risk Solutions** claims "85% of transaction laundering cases come from merchants that appear low risk" (vendor claim). Source: https://g2risksolutions.com/merchant-risk/product/transaction-laundering-detection
- **G2 Risk Solutions** says it covers "100+ high-risk areas" including scam networks and transaction laundering (vendor claim). Source: https://g2risksolutions.com/merchant-risk-solutions
- **Mastercard** MATCH Pro defines reason code 03, "Transaction Laundering", as a MATCH merchant submitting transactions that did not arise from a Merchant Agreement between acquirer and merchant. Source: https://www.mastercard.com/content/dam/mccom/shared/business/support/rules-pdfs/SPME-Manual.pdf
- **Mastercard** BRAM (Business Risk Assessment and Mitigation) covers illegal and brand-damaging activity. Its Merchant Monitoring Program (MMP) registers service providers that offer BRAM content monitoring and transaction-laundering detection, and Mastercard has provided assessment mitigation where acquirers used such monitoring. Source: https://docs.nuvei.com/documentation/security-docs/risk-guide/schemes-programs/
- **Mastercard** BRAM fine range: a secondary source cites $5,000 to $200,000 per violation; not verified against Mastercard's manuals. Source: https://ballerine.com/glossary/mastercard-bram-business-risk-assessment-and-mitigation-program
- **Visa** VAMP (Visa Acquirer Monitoring Program) replaced the separate VDMP and VFMP programs. Its ratio is (fraud TC40 + disputes TC15) / settled transactions TC05, with an excessive-merchant threshold of 220 bps. Source: https://corporate.visa.com/content/dam/VCOM/corporate/visa-perspectives/security-and-trust/documents/visa-acquirer-monitoring-program-fact-sheet-2025.pdf
- **Visa** VIRP is described as the successor to GBPP, covering BRAM-type violations. Source: https://www.legitscript.com/bram-virp/
- **Visa** transaction-laundering detection vendor programme: not found. VAMP sources cover fraud and dispute ratios, not laundering detection.
- **Evertec / EVO**: Evertec's fraud product page lists tokenization, 3DS authentication, and RiskCenter 360 real-time analysis. No transaction-laundering product was found. Source: https://evertecinc.com/en/solution/fraud-prevention/
- **EverC** (a separate vendor, not Evertec) offers "MerchantView", which claims to detect transaction laundering and high-risk merchants with AI plus analysts (vendor claim). Source: https://www.about-fraud.com/providers/everc/
- **Ekata**: acquired by Mastercard for US$850 million (announced April 2021, closed 9 June 2021). Ekata is a digital identity-verification vendor, not a laundering detector. Sources: https://investor.mastercard.com/investor-news/investor-news-details/2021/Mastercard-to-Acquire-Ekata-to-Advance-Digital-Identity-Efforts/default.aspx and https://katten.com/katten-advises-ekata-shareholders-on-acquisition-by-mastercard
- **India targeting**: not found for the transaction-laundering vendors above. LegitScript and G2 pages do not name India. Mastercard and Visa programmes are global.

## Q2. India players for merchant risk and onboarding

- **IDfy** Payment Acquirers page lists merchant onboarding, merchant monitoring, and cross/up-selling. It claims to be "fully compliant with master directions on payment aggregators 2025", 200+ fraud and risk signals for antecedent checks, and "90% accuracy using shop images or merchant website" (vendor claim). Source: https://www.idfy.com/industries/payments/acquirers/
- **Cashfree** RiskShield is described as post-activation payment-risk controls that complement onboarding due diligence. Source: https://www.cashfree.com/blog/merchant-onboarding-process/ (product link: https://www.cashfree.com/risk-shield-payment-gateway/)
- **Cashfree** describes ongoing monitoring as checking whether payment activity matches the merchant's declared profile, limits, and expected behaviour (company description). Source: https://www.cashfree.com/blog/merchant-onboarding-process/
- **Razorpay Thirdwatch** is an e-commerce fraud product that scores users, orders, and addresses, and flags impulse purchases, non-deliverable addresses, and COD/RTO fraud, using ML and cross-website history. It is not described as a merchant-laundering product. Sources: https://razorpay.com/blog/buyer-action-thirdwatch/ and https://razorpay.com/blog/thirdwatch-ecommerce-fraud-webinar-summary/
- **Signzy** offers business verification (registered documents, government databases, UBO checks), KYC, and AML "transaction monitoring", described as AI-first AML. Source: https://www.signzy.com/
- **Juspay**: company describes "risk management and security" tooling and fraud checks inside its orchestration flow. No merchant-laundering feature was found. Sources: https://juspay.io/en-in/blog/everything-you-need-to-know-about-payment-aggregator and https://juspay.io/sea/docs/hyper-checkout-sea/ios/overview/payment-orchestrator-flow
- **PayU**: blog describes automated fraud checks before approval and regulatory monitoring. No merchant-laundering feature was found. Source: https://payu.in/blog/what-is-payment-aggregator/
- **Bureau (bureau.id)**, **Perfios**, **Setu**, **Gupshup**: not found in searches.

## Q4. Indian regulation

- **RBI Master Direction on Regulation of Payment Aggregators (PA)**, page marked "Updated as on November 11, 2026". It defines PA-P (physical) and sets due-diligence requirements. Source: https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_ViewMasDirections.aspx?id=12896
- The MD requires a PA to undertake customer due diligence (CDD) of its merchants under the KYC MD. Where a PA contracts another PA that onboards the merchant, the latter is responsible for due diligence. Source: https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_ViewMasDirections.aspx?id=12896
- The MD requires the PA to monitor transactions the merchant later undertakes, to ensure they are in line with the merchant's business profile. Source: https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_ViewMasDirections.aspx?id=12896
- Transition: merchants onboarded up to 31 December 2025 must comply with the due-diligence requirements within one year of the MD. From 1 January 2026, merchants must be onboarded under the MD. Source: https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_ViewMasDirections.aspx?id=12896
- The MD requires a non-bank PA to register with FIU-IND. Source: https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_ViewMasDirections.aspx?id=12896
- The Directions are titled "Reserve Bank of India (Regulation of Payment Aggregators) Directions, 2025". PA-P-only entities had to apply for a Certificate of Authorisation by 31 December 2025, or wind down. Sources: https://littdb.sfo2.cdn.digitaloceanspaces.com/litt/IN/RBI/master_directions/RBI_MD_12896.html?ref=legal-wires.com and https://www.khaitanco.com/sites/default/files/2025-10/ERGO%20-%20PA%20Master%20Directions%20-%203%20Oct%202025.pdf
- Law-firm summary: "Continuous transaction monitoring of the merchant to be undertaken, to ensure transactions are in line with merchant's business profile." Source: https://www.cyrilshroff.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/Client-Alert-RBI-Introduces-Consolidated-Framework-for-Payment-Aggregators-3.pdf
- **MuleHunter.AI** (RBI Innovation Hub, RBIH): AI/ML platform to identify mule accounts used to move or launder money from cyber fraud. Announced by RBI on 6 December 2024. Free to banks, per bankers quoted. Source: https://bfsi.economictimes.indiatimes.com/articles/rbih-promotes-mulehunterai-for-combatting-cyber-fraud-in-banking/131185297
- **MuleHunter.AI** adoption: about 28 to 30 banks reported integrated (executive quote, ET BFSI). Source: https://bfsi.economictimes.indiatimes.com/articles/rbih-promotes-mulehunterai-for-combatting-cyber-fraud-in-banking/131185297
- **MuleHunter.AI** accuracy claim: "85%+ accuracy" (RBIH project page, vendor claim). Source: https://rbihub.in/projects/mulehunter
- **I4C-RBIH MoU**: I4C (MHA) and RBIH signed an MoU to feed I4C's Suspect Registry into MuleHunter.ai and related AI fraud-detection systems. Source: https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2260277&reg=3&lang=2
- **NPCI UPI merchant risk rules** (betting-specific): not found. The NPCI circulars page surfaced no betting or gambling rule. Source checked: https://www.npci.org.in/circulars/upi
- **Promotion and Regulation of Online Gaming Act, 2025**: Section 5 prohibits offering or operating an online money game or online money gaming service. Section 7 bars any transaction or authorisation of funds for an online money gaming service, including by banks or payment systems. Section 14 provides for blocking. Advertising is also prohibited. Sources: https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/10/8a7f103cefc68ed8aaa2ebc9a2ed7c13.pdf and https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2241804&reg=3&lang=1
- **Implementation status conflict**: a Lexology article describes the Act as "yet to be implemented". Its status as of November 2026 was not confirmed. Source: https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=31a466da-49b3-40b5-9767-2ff42367d307
- **Blocking activity (secondary sources, 2026)**: a Government order blocked 242 illegal betting sites (reported 16 November 2026). A 15m.com article reports 8,376 betting/gambling URLs blocked by March 2026. Sources: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sVxabmOErzI and https://15m.com/articles/payments-are-the-new-choke-point-how-india-and-indonesia-are-hunting-grey-market-operators/

## Q5. Known Indian cases (illegal betting and mule laundering)

- **Mahadev Online Book / Mahadev betting app**: app created in 2018, operated from Dubai through a franchise of "betting panels" on a 70:30 profit split, with player money through bank accounts held in others' names and out of India via hawala. Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahadev_Betting_App_Case and https://www.thehindu.com/news/national/after-cbi-ed-arrests-mahadev-betting-app-scam-accused/article71699161.ece
- **ED arrest**: Saurabh Chandrakar, co-promoter, arrested by ED on 13 November 2026 (The Hindu, 14 November 2026). Source: https://www.thehindu.com/news/national/after-cbi-ed-arrests-mahadev-betting-app-scam-accused/article71699161.ece
- **ED figures to date** (per ED official, The Hindu): about 190 premises searched; about ₹2,000 crore seized or frozen; about ₹3,500 crore attached, of which about ₹1,800 crore abroad (including 22 properties in Dubai); six prosecution complaints against 116 accused. Source: https://www.thehindu.com/news/national/after-cbi-ed-arrests-mahadev-betting-app-scam-accused/article71699161.ece
- **Vikas Garg**: ED arrested him in July 2026. His companies reportedly received about ₹940 crore of betting proceeds, of which about ₹766 crore went into the acquisition of Ebix Inc. ED has attached about ₹941 crore in that limb. Source: https://www.thehindu.com/news/national/after-cbi-ed-arrests-mahadev-betting-app-scam-accused/article71699161.ece
- **Fresh attachment**: ED attached assets worth about ₹388 crore across India, Dubai and Mauritius (date not verified). Source: https://www.pgurus.com/mahadev-app-betting-case-ed-attaches-fresh-assets-worth-rs-388-crore-across-india-dubai-mauritius/
- **UPI or payment-aggregator merchant accounts in the Mahadev case**: not found in the sources reviewed. The reports describe bank accounts in others' names and hawala, not PA merchant accounts.
- **FIU-IND penalty on Bybit Fintech**: ₹9 crore 27 lakh on a Virtual Digital Asset Service Provider (January 2025). This is a crypto VASP, not a PA. Source: https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2098153&reg=48&lang=2
- **FIU-IND penalty on Paytm Payments Bank**: ₹5.49 crore for alleged money-laundering violations (secondary LinkedIn post, not verified against an FIU-IND order). Source: https://www.linkedin.com/posts/rushda-khan-02650657_aml-pmla-penalties-activity-7402673022920503298-Eo41
- **PayPal Payments v. FIU-IND** (court challenge to a PMLA penalty): the court observed that FIU-IND had not recorded justification for the maximum penalty. Source: https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=c6895fff-79d0-474a-bbda-c93afabbbf05
- **ED actions against PAs specifically**: not found.

## Q6. Gaps, criticism, and scale

- **Cloaking**: a Google research paper ("Cloak of Visibility") found cloaking against crawlers is common. 4.9% of ads cloaked against Googlebot in the paper's measurement. Its multi-crawler approach detected cloaking 95.5% of the time, with a 0.9% false-positive rate. Source: https://research.google.com/pubs/archive/45365.pdf
- **Crawler false positives** (same paper): sources were content revised between crawls, crawl timing and page-load failures, and Cloudflare DDoS challenges returning interstitials. Source: https://research.google.com/pubs/archive/45365.pdf
- **Geo-fencing, app-only betting, Telegram or WhatsApp-distributed links**: not found as published criticisms of crawling-based detection in the sources reviewed. Partial relevance: the Mahadev reporting describes the operation as distributed through franchised "panels", not websites. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahadev_Betting_App_Case
- **Analyst-led alternative**: LegitScript says its analysts confirm laundering "before the card network" (vendor claim). Source: https://www.legitscript.com/solutions/merchant-risk-solutions/transaction-laundering-detection/
- **Scale of transaction laundering**: no independent global estimate found. Vendor-only figures: G2 says "85% of transaction laundering cases come from merchants that appear low risk" (vendor claim). LegitScript says it saves clients "tens of millions of dollars" in fines (vendor claim). Sources: https://g2risksolutions.com/merchant-risk/product/transaction-laundering-detection and https://www.legitscript.com/solutions/merchant-risk-solutions/merchant-monitoring/
- **False-positive burden (general AML, not transaction laundering)**: a vendor (Flagright) states that up to 95% of AML alerts are false positives, citing a generic industry estimate. Not verified. Source: https://www.flagright.com/post/understanding-false-positives-in-transaction-monitoring
- **Card-not-present fraud context**: the Federal Reserve reports card-not-present transactions at 34.4% of total volume in 2023. Source: https://www.federalreserve.gov/paymentsystems/files/debitfees_costs_2023.pdf

## Thin areas

- Q2: Bureau, Perfios, Setu, and Gupshup have no merchant-monitoring source. Razorpay and Juspay sources describe e-commerce or general fraud tooling, not laundering.
- Q4: NPCI betting rules, and the current in-force status of the Online Gaming Act, were not confirmed.
- Q5: No case found tying a PA or UPI merchant account to a betting collection, other than the general ED narrative.
- Q6: No published source on geo-fencing, app-only, or Telegram/WhatsApp-distributed betting limits on crawling detection.
